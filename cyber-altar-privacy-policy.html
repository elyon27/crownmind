1. Who we are and what this policy covers

Cyber-Altar is an online prayer sanctuary operated by CrownMind Institute (referred to as “we,” “us,” or “our”). This policy explains how we handle information when you use the Cyber-Altar Android app, package net.crownmind.cyberaltar, and its companion website at cyber-altar.netlify.app.

The Android app displays the live website within an Android WebView. An internet connection is required. Information entered through the app is processed by the same online services used by the website. The app and an external browser have separate local storage and sign-in sessions.

3. How we use information

We use information to provide prayer and altar features, verify account access, retain your selections, display public content, manage candle lifetimes, provide support, and prevent misuse. We do not sell personal information or use prayer content for targeted advertising.

The supplied Android release contains no advertising SDK or dedicated analytics SDK. It does not request the Android advertising ID, contacts, camera, microphone, SMS, precise location, or broad file-storage access. It uses the internet permission to connect to Cyber-Altar. Network services still receive the technical information needed to handle those connections.

4. Private prayers, public prayers, and the Pilgrims directory

  • Private prayers: New prayers are stored as private until you choose to publish them. Private prayers are intended for your verified account’s Holy Place, rather than the Public Prayer Wall. They are stored on our backend and are not end-to-end encrypted; authorized administration and service providers may process them where necessary to operate, secure, or support the service.
  • Public Prayer Wall: Choosing “Paste Prayer On Wall” or the equivalent publishing control makes the prayer text and associated display information, such as your pilgrim name, altar, candle activity, and prayer time, available to other visitors. Visitors may be able to view the wall without signing in.
  • Changing visibility: Choosing “Keep Secret” or the equivalent private control removes the prayer from the public wall in the service. It does not delete the stored prayer or copies that someone else may already have made.
  • Pilgrims directory: Enrolled pilgrim names, including names drawn from older altar registrations, are listed in the Pilgrims directory. Your directory entry does not depend on whether your prayers are public. The directory does not display your email address or private prayers. Contact us if you want your enrollment and directory entry removed.

Public material may be copied, shared, cached, or captured in screenshots by others. We cannot guarantee removal of copies held outside Cyber-Altar.

5. Local storage, cookies, and device data

Cyber-Altar uses browser/WebView local storage and authentication storage, including cookies where applicable, to retain your pilgrim name, email, selected altar, altar history, candle-related state, and sign-in session. WebView may also cache web resources. These records help the app function and recognize your previous activity.

The Android release disables Android cloud backup for its app data. You can remove app-local information through Android Settings → Apps → Cyber-Altar → Storage → Clear storage or Clear data. Uninstalling normally removes app-local data. Menu names vary by device. These actions do not delete your online account or server records. Clearing cache alone may not remove saved preferences or sessions.

6. Service providers and other disclosures

Cyber-Altar uses Netlify for website hosting and server functionality and Supabase for database and authentication services. Authentication email delivery also involves the configured email delivery infrastructure and your email provider. These providers process information needed to perform their services. Their infrastructure may be located outside your country.

Relevant provider policies include Netlify’s Privacy Statement and Supabase’s Privacy Policy. Google Play and Android may separately process installation, testing, feedback, or diagnostic information under their own policies and your device settings.

We may disclose information when required by applicable law or a valid legal process, or as necessary to investigate abuse and protect the service or others. Access to nonpublic information should be limited to authorized persons and providers who need it for these purposes.

7. Security

The app connects to Cyber-Altar using HTTPS. The Android release blocks cleartext web traffic and mixed HTTP content, disables local file/content access in WebView, and does not expose a native JavaScript bridge. Email verification and backend access controls help protect private records.

No online service or device can guarantee complete security. Keep your device secure, do not share verification codes, and contact us if you believe your account has been accessed without authorization.

8. How long information is retained

Enrollment and account records are retained while needed to provide your account and associated services. Prayers and altar history remain until removed through an available feature or a verified deletion request, subject to limited retention necessary for security, resolving disputes, or legal obligations.

A candle’s displayed burning period lasts approximately 24 hours. Its expiry and removal from display are not a promise that all associated prayer, account, historical, or technical records are immediately deleted.

Technical logs, support records, and backup copies may have separate retention periods. Backup copies may remain until replaced or expired under the relevant provider’s retention processes. Retained information should be restricted to its necessary purpose; a completed deletion request must not be used to recreate an active account from a backup.

9. Your choices and requests to delete your account

You can change your pilgrim name through the entrance’s name-change option, choose whether to publish a prayer, and use available altar-removal controls after verifying your email. Removing an altar does not by itself delete your entire enrollment or authentication account.

Request account and associated data deletion:

  1. Email contact@crownmind.net with the subject Cyber-Altar account deletion.
  2. Send the request from your enrolled email address where possible, and include your pilgrim name. If you cannot access that address, explain the situation so we can consider another reasonable way to verify ownership.
  3. State whether you want your entire account and associated data deleted, or only specified records.

Full deletion requests cover your enrollment, authentication account, pilgrim directory entry, altar profiles, prayers, and associated candle records. We may verify ownership before taking action and will explain any information that must be retained and why. You can submit this request without reinstalling the app.

You may also contact us to request access, correction, or information about the handling of your personal data, or to raise a privacy concern. Applicable rights depend on your location and circumstances. Do not send passwords, verification codes, identity documents, or private prayer text unless specifically necessary and requested through an appropriate process.

10. Children’s privacy

Cyber-Altar is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly seek personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has submitted personal information, contact us so we can review and remove it as appropriate. Parents and guardians should supervise younger users’ online activity and prevent public sharing of children’s personal information.

11. External links

Links to CrownMind or other websites may open outside the app. Those services have their own privacy practices. Cyber-Altar does not control information you provide to an external destination, and this policy does not replace that destination’s privacy policy.

12. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as the service or its data practices change. The date at the top identifies the latest revision. Material changes should be communicated through the app or website and, where required, an appropriate notice or consent process.
